# Executive summary: why growth stalled and what to do about it

*Olist marketplace, real public data, 97,905 orders from Jan 2017 to Aug 2018. All figures come from `reports/facts.json`.*

## The situation

The marketplace sold R$13.4M of goods in twenty months. On paper growth is strong: Jan-Aug revenue was up
138% on the same months of 2017. That comparison flatters the business, because 2017 was the
ramp-up year. In 2018 monthly revenue stopped climbing: it averaged R$896k a month in May-Aug,
4.7% below Jan-Apr.

## Why

1. **Almost nobody comes back.** Only 3.0% of customers ever placed a second order, and repeat orders are
   2.9% of revenue. Nearly every real of revenue comes from a first-time buyer.
2. **New-customer growth has stopped.** First-time customers levelled off at about 6,459 a month in 2018.
   With flat acquisition and no retention, revenue goes flat.
3. **Basket size is not growing either.** Average order value moved 0.4% year on year, and only
   9.9% of orders contain more than one item.
4. **Late deliveries damage the experience.** 6.8% of delivered orders arrive late. Those orders average
   2.27 stars against 4.29 for on-time orders. The 14 states with above-average
   late rates receive 30% of deliveries but 51% of late ones; Rio de Janeiro alone
   accounts for 23%.

## What to do

| Action | Who it targets | Estimated value per year |
|---|---|---|
| Second-purchase programme | 28,779 high-value one-time buyers (Promising, At-Risk) | R$95.1k at +1.0 pp repeat rate |
| Fix delivery promises | 14 states above the national late rate | 1,477 fewer late orders, +0.04 average stars |
| Cross-sell bundles | All checkouts in top categories | R$179k at +2.0 pp multi-item share |

Together the three actions are worth about **R$275k a year**, or 2.5% of the
2018 run-rate. That is a modest number, and it should be read honestly: the lifts are assumptions to be tested, not promises.
The bigger point is structural. A marketplace where 97% of buyers never return cannot grow faster than its
acquisition budget, so retention has to become a managed metric.

## Recommended next steps

1. Launch the second-purchase programme as an A/B test on next month's Promising and At-Risk customers; read repeat rate after 180 days.
2. Agree delivery-promise targets per state with the logistics team; track on-time rate weekly on the dashboard.
3. Add a "bought together" module in the top five categories and measure multi-item share.
4. Add product cost to the data so the next analysis can rank categories by profit, not revenue.
